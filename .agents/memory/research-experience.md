## Ongoing research monitoring

The user wants saved research ideas, literature surveys, journal drafts, theses and topics monitored daily for related journal publications and published patents.

**Why:** They want to learn when others publish research related to their planned work, not only when they manually repeat a search.

**How to apply:** Keep monitoring project-based to avoid duplicate alerts across related drafts. Explain substantive overlap without asserting copying or loss of novelty; distinguish publication dates from discovery dates and unavailable patent coverage from a successful search with no matches.

## Research workflow

The user expects scientific figures and diagrams as part of journal papers, theses and research drafts wherever appropriate, and wants AI Chat to insert them on request.

**Why:** The user corrected the earlier implication that text generation and formatted exports alone fulfilled complete research-document generation.

**How to apply:** Treat source-grounded plots, theoretical calculations, conceptual diagrams and their document exports as part of the research workflow. Do not describe a text-only draft as complete. Never manufacture experimental observations to satisfy a figure request.

The user wants PDF/Word review inside AI Chat, with coloured suggestions on the right, hover explanations for text and diagram changes, and explicit approval before applying changes.

**Why:** They want to inspect what needs changing and retain control over each correction rather than receive an automatically rewritten document.

**How to apply:** Preserve the uploaded original and earlier revisions. Pair colour with readable labels and keyboard/touch-accessible explanations. Distinguish retrieved paper/patent evidence and inspected visuals from unsearched or unrendered material.

The user wants supplementary Internet search wherever papers are searched across the app, to help find research gaps.

**Why:** They want wider context beyond scholarly catalogues and patents, while keeping research quality and costs important.

**How to apply:** Include Internet evidence in paper-search and research-generation workflows, while respecting explicit project-only or no-new-search instructions. Do not represent missing Internet results as proof of novelty.

The user wants research to be very easy, with a direct question-to-answer flow and instant answers; they report that the current flow takes too long.

The user wants generated responses to stream visibly across all pages rather than appear only after completion.

**Why:** Waiting for the full response makes the app feel unresponsive.

**How to apply:** Show genuine generated text as it arrives, distinguish unfinished drafts from checked final output, and retain progress indicators during retrieval when no response text exists yet.

**Why:** The user identified waiting time as their main problem, rather than the choice of vector database.

**How to apply:** Prioritize end-to-end answer latency and simple interactions when evaluating architecture. Preserve the existing accuracy requirement; distinguish progress updates or provisional content from evidence-checked answers, and do not promise instant full-paper analysis.

The user wants numbered citations, a polished research interface, and research-related AI-agent names, using the supplied AnswerThis portal screenshots as a workflow reference.

**Why:** They explicitly requested numbering, better UI/UX, and named research agents after reviewing the competitor's answer/source and processing panels.

**How to apply:** Keep citations connected to their sources and research-role labels tied to actual processing. Design previews must not be presented as functioning new agents or measured speed improvements.

Show processing in a popup with five colour-coded research-agent roles, tailored to the feature on each page, including names such as “Literature Survey Agent.”

**Why:** The user explicitly replaced the inline processing-bar preference with a five-agent popup, using a screenshot from their other product as the reference.

**How to apply:** Drive the active role from reported backend stages, not timed simulations; distinguish workflow position from estimated completion time and mark skipped discovery roles honestly for same-paper follow-ups. Keep live text available and use the same popup pattern across processing features.

Publisher-specific requests such as “focus on IEEE publications from 2020 onward” are hard evidence restrictions, not just ranking preferences.

**Why:** The user flagged an MDPI paper in the evidence list for that IEEE-specific question.

**How to apply:** Restrict discovered, selected and cited papers consistently; exclude missing required publisher/year metadata instead of silently broadening the request. Keep historical answers intact and clearly flag answers generated before filtering was enforced.

Research answers should read as professional academic syntheses: organised evidence-based paragraphs, numbered citations, references and concise scientific limitations—not lists of internal processing notices. Default to expanded explanations of mechanisms, study conditions, measured results and defensible comparisons rather than a few isolated findings. Confirmed ideas should be developed research briefs, not a few one-line fields.

**Why:** The user requested university-quality research writing after seeing embedding, database and internal-ID details mixed into the answer, explicitly chose “Expand research answers,” and rejected thin confirmed-idea content.

**How to apply:** Separate reproducibility/processing notes from the scientific narrative. Follow clear claim–evidence–analysis structure without implying Harvard endorsement, inventing research methods, or padding unsupported sections. Preserve verified claims and user-edited drafts when improving older presentations.

For expanded answers, word counts are targets, not minimums. Ask for distinct supported aspects in focused paragraphs rather than one long paragraph per paper.

**Why:** Whole-paragraph evidence checking can discard an otherwise useful discussion when one assertion is unsupported; requesting a longer total answer alone did not reliably produce a developed synthesis.

**How to apply:** Plan section and paragraph coverage, preserve independently supported material, and permit shorter outputs when evidence cannot support more detail. Never relax citation matching or repeatedly regenerate just to reach a word count.

The user approved the ivory/teal Research Studio design and asked to use it, with as many papers as practical shown on the right.

**Why:** They said “I think this is best design use it but right side show as much as papers.”

**How to apply:** Preserve the approved reading-and-evidence layout. The user also wants minimal top whitespace and conversation history moved out of the main content flow. Show retrieved papers separately from those selected or cited; never imply that a larger discovery list means every paper was read.

Paper details should expand below the clicked sidebar paper, without moving the page upward.

**Why:** The user explicitly requested downward expansion instead of an inspector above the list.

**How to apply:** Keep sidebar clicks in place; citation links may reveal their corresponding source.

The user wants processing options enabled by default and no repeated OpenAI-sending consent or PDF-page inspection switches anywhere in the app.

**Why:** They explicitly asked to remove these controls and make the available processing automatic.

**How to apply:** Keep the question-to-answer flow simple, without per-message consent checkboxes. Preserve source-selection boundaries, ownership checks, budget limits and truthful evidence limitations.

Default freshness policy: “Recent first, allow older.”

**Why:** The user chose this over excluding everything outside the last 60 days after noticing references from 2012–2022.

**How to apply:** Prioritise relevant newer publications in discovery and selection, retain useful older evidence, and distinguish the report date from source publication dates. A year alone is not proof of publication in the last two months; indexing delays prevent guarantees of complete coverage through yesterday. Do not imply a fresh search when reusing historical papers.

When usage limits are exhausted, the user wants this shown across the app, and clicking a restricted action should explain the limit rather than appear unresponsive.

**Why:** They explicitly asked for “limit reached” feedback everywhere when attempting to click.

**How to apply:** Distinguish account allowances from the shared service AI budget, preserve unlimited-account exemptions only for account quotas, and keep viewing saved research available.

The users are non-technical and want as few steps as possible; a visible six-step research journey feels overwhelming.

**Why:** The user explicitly rejected the six-step presentation of the connected design previews as too much for their audience.

**How to apply:** Keep research capabilities available without presenting a mandatory six-stage process. The user approved one chat-first workspace with conversation, evidence and writing as contextual states, not separate steps. Reveal sources and an editable draft alongside the conversation only when needed.

The user specifically approved the simplified writing preview: conversation on the left, editable working draft on the right, and sources available on demand.

**Why:** They supplied the writing-state screenshot and said “this is good.”

**How to apply:** Use this side-by-side composition as the preferred writing direction. Keep the conversation available while editing, without restoring a visible multi-step journey.

The user now requests persistent navigation across AI Chat, Literature Survey, Synopsis, Journal Papers, Thesis, AI Plagiarism and Patents. AI Chat should offer five research ideas in a coloured popup with confirmation on each and back/forward navigation. Papers and relevant patents should support all research modules.

**Why:** The user reported that other sidebar pages disappear in AI Chat and explicitly described this revised product flow.

**How to apply:** This supersedes hiding modules inside the chat-only shell. Keep modules independently accessible, preserve shared project context, and avoid forcing all users through every page. Journal and university templates, patent integration and integrity checking remain requested capabilities until implemented and verified.

The user wants one project per My Library folder, usable from every research page. Ideas, references and generated documents should be reusable through requests such as “my idea” and “my references,” with PDF/Word export and Save to Library available from each creation page. They request Weaviate storage for project knowledge.

**Why:** The user wants to start anywhere without re-uploading or repeating project information.

**How to apply:** Preserve a selected project context across pages and ask when a reference is ambiguous. Treat Weaviate as searchable project knowledge, with original/exported files linked from durable file storage; explain this distinction before implementation. Enforce project and user boundaries and preserve draft versions.

All research responses and drafts should use professional, natural academic prose rather than generic AI-style writing.

**Why:** The user explicitly requests “professional human writing format” across literature surveys, journal papers and theses, with checking tools where appropriate.

**How to apply:** Check clarity, evidence, citations, consistency and similarity without claiming AI-generated text is human-authored or guaranteeing AI-detector outcomes.

The user wants topic entry and document upload to be prominent, not squeezed into a narrow column beside a large unused placeholder.

**Why:** They rejected the Ideas screen because important controls and uploads were pushed below the fold while the empty output area took most of the width.

**How to apply:** Prioritize the action users need before generation; keep empty states and secondary settings compact, with useful input space on laptop screens. After selection, show the idea or answer at the top in a wide conversation-style workspace, not beneath the original large input form. Keep follow-up entry compact and ancillary tools on demand.
